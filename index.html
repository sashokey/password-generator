Length and strength are separate

Use 15–128 characters for password-only sign-in, or 8–128 with MFA. Short word passwords can still be weak; Random characters provides more possibilities per character.

Security model and standards+

The length profiles implement the password-length guidance in NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.1.2: 15+ characters for single-factor sign-in, or 8+ when a password is used with MFA. This is a length check, not a certification or a guarantee of password strength. MFA must actually be enabled on the account.

Randomness comes exclusively from Web Crypto. Generation requires HTTPS, localhost, or a trusted local file. If secure randomness fails, no password is produced. Passwords are not sent, saved, or logged by this app; copying puts the selected value on your system clipboard.

Word mode samples uniformly across valid phrases, number/symbol positions, and initial-letter cases for the chosen length. Whole words and their order are preserved. Character mode samples uniformly from the selected alphabet, conditioned on including each selected group at least once. These groups are your optional generation preferences, not mandatory account password rules.

search-space bits = log₂(number of possible generated outputs)

Fixed grammar words and separators add no randomness. Random layouts and case choices do. The displayed number is the size of the search space under the random-source assumption; it is not a certification of the browser's cryptographic strength or a cracking-time estimate. The 80-bit target is this app's advisory threshold, not a NIST requirement. The comparison uses 94 uniformly random printable non-space ASCII characters and rounds down.

Full authentication-system conformity also requires server-side compromised-password screening, throttling, secure password hashing, and other controls. This static generator cannot verify those controls, certify an AAL, or establish FIPS validation of your browser. See OWASP authentication guidance.

Expanded vocabulary includes rare and technical terms. Familiar keeps EFF words and words with repeated occurrences in the WordNet tagged corpus. Story mode uses semantic categories and grammar templates; short lengths use descriptive pairs or single words.
